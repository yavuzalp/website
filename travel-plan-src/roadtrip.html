You were right — Crater Lake wasn't in V1 or V2 at all

When we moved the dark-sky destination from Crater Lake to the Oregon Outback, I dropped Crater Lake from the route entirely instead of keeping it as a stop. That was a mistake and you caught it. It's back, as night 3.

It genuinely needs its own night: it sits ~100 miles south of Bend on US-97, and there is no direct road east from it to Summer Lake. There's no cheap detour version. So this adds one day and the trip now ends Monday Sept 14 instead of Sunday the 13th.

The upside: adding a day makes the stargazing better

Shifting the Outback from Sept 6–7 to Sept 7–8 moves you closer to the new moon, and the waning crescent rises later each night. Plus Crater Lake's own rim sits at 7,000 ft with genuinely dark skies, so night 3 becomes a bonus.

Three consecutive dark nights instead of two, and the best one is now 7 hours 42 minutes of completely moonless sky — up from 6h22m in V2. Three nights also pushes your odds of catching at least one genuinely clear night past 98%.

Know this before you commit: Cleetwood Cove is closed until 2029

The Cleetwood Cove Trail — the only legal access to the lake's shoreline — is being fully rebuilt across the 2026, 2027 and 2028 summer seasons. That means no boat tours, no Wizard Island shuttle, no swimming, no lakeshore access at all while you're there.

Everything else is open and operating normally: all 33 miles of Rim Drive with its 30+ pullouts, every rim trail including Garfield Peak and Watchman Peak, both visitor centers, the lodge, ranger programs, and Mazama Campground. Roughly 90% of visitors experience the park entirely from the rim anyway — that's where the view is.

If the boat tour was the specific draw, it's worth knowing it returns in 2029. If you want the rim views, nothing has changed.

Crater LakeRim Drive · Garfield Peak · Mazama Village
NEW135 mi🌒 26% · dark 5h›
Route

US-97 south from Bend past Chemult to Diamond Lake Junction, then OR-138 west and down the North Entrance Road to the caldera. ~120 mi to the rim, plus however much of Rim Drive you take.

Budget the whole day. The lake is 1,943 ft deep — the deepest in the United States, ninth deepest in the world — and it holds nothing but snowmelt, which is why the blue looks fake.

  • Rim Drive — 33 miles, 30+ pullouts. Do the full circuit
  • Garfield Peak — 8,054 ft, widely considered the best hike in the park, 3.4 mi round trip from Rim Village
  • Watchman Peak — the iconic Wizard Island view, 1.6 mi round trip
  • Sinnott Memorial Overlook — stone platform carved into the caldera wall below Rim Village
  • Pinnacles and Plaikni Falls off the southeast rim if you have time
Layers. The rim is above 7,000 ft and runs 35–40°F colder than the valleys below. A warm day in Medford is a breezy 55°F up here. Also pack lunch — in-park dining is just the lodge and Mazama Village.
Charging

There is no fast charging at Crater Lake. The park has a 16 kW destination charger and Crater Lake Resort in Fort Klamath has a 13 kW unit — both are overnight-trickle speed, not useful for a fill.

This is fine because of the routing: leave Bend at 100%, drive 135 mi including Rim Drive, and arrive at Mazama around 50–55%. Tomorrow's first stop is a Supercharger 55 miles away. Plug into the park's destination charger overnight if a stall is free — it's free margin.

Camp

Mazama Village Campground — 7 mi south of the rim, 214 sites (121 tent-only), ~6,000 ft. Picnic table and fire ring per site, flush toilets, coin showers, potable water, camp store and laundry. $35/night for a tent site.

Reservations are required July 1 – Oct 1, 2026 via recreation.gov, 6-month rolling window — so you're chasing cancellations here like everywhere else. Check daily.

Backup 1 Diamond Lake Campground, Umpqua NF, 20 mi north
Backup 2 Collier Memorial State Park, 30 mi south on US-97
Backup 3 Crater Lake Resort, Fort Klamath — private, has the 13 kW charger

Tonight

Sunset 7:38 pm · full dark 9:12 pm · moon doesn't rise until 2:13 am. Five moonless hours at 6,000 ft.

Crater Lake isn't Bortle 1 like the Outback, but it's a long way from anything and the park runs ranger astronomy programs in summer. Worth walking up to a rim pullout after dark — the caldera under the Milky Way is a different thing entirely.

4Mon 9/7

210 miKlamath Falls SC🌒 16.5% · dark 6h22m›
Route

South out of the park on OR-62 through Fort Klamath, down to Klamath Falls (55 mi), then east on OR-140 across the Gearhart Wilderness edge through Bly to Lakeview (95 mi), then northwest on OR-31 through Paisley to Summer Lake Hot Springs (55 mi).

This southern approach is new in this version, and it's better than doubling back up US-97. You get the Klamath Basin, a genuinely remote stretch of OR-140, and you enter the Dark Sky Sanctuary through its southern gateway at Lakeview rather than the northern one.

Charging

SuperchargerKlamath Falls — and there are now two: 2655 Shasta Way (8 stalls, 150 kW) and N Eldorado Ave / College Square (12 stalls, newer, right by the US-97 merge with food nearby).

Charge to 100% at Klamath Falls. It's 150 mi from there to Summer Lake Hot Springs, so you arrive around 45–50% with plenty in hand — and this is the last fast charging until Eugene, three days out.

East of Klamath Falls there is effectively nothing. Lakeview has a Level 2 at the Ford dealership on OR-140 and that's it; the only other charger in Lake County is a Level 2 at a hardware store in Christmas Valley, $2/hr, 9–5.

Camp

Summer Lake Hot Springs — OR-31, 6 mi west of Paisley. (541) 943-3931. Two nights.

  • ~12 tent sites plus a 5-acre dry camping field. $20 per person per night. Open flat ground, fire pits, no tables, no trees
  • Book a 20–50 amp hookup site (+$20/night) and tent on it. Ford Mobile Power Cord into the NEMA 14-50 gives ~7.7 kW, about 17 miles of range per hour — roughly 200 mi back per overnight. Two nights and you leave full
  • Geothermal soaking pools on site

Free alternative Summer Lake Wildlife Area — four primitive ODFW campgrounds, free with a $10/day permit that is not sold on site. No water, no power. Darker; use it for viewing, sleep where the outlet is.

Full dark 9:04 pm → moonrise 3:26 am. 6 hours 22 minutes moonless, under the darkest certified sky in the country.

The place

Certified by DarkSky International in March 2024, the Oregon Outback International Dark Sky Sanctuary covers 2.5 million acres of Lake County — the largest dark sky sanctuary in the world. Roughly 15,000 stars visible on a clear night, against about 500 from a city.

Daytime
  • Summer Lake Wildlife Area auto tour route — 8,800 acres of marsh under the 3,000 ft Winter Ridge escarpment. Scout tonight's viewing spot here
  • Paisley — the Pioneer Saloon, one of the oldest bars in Oregon
  • Chewaucan River south of Paisley — swimming holes, Marster Spring
  • Fort Rock — a 300 ft volcanic tuff ring standing alone in flat desert, 65 mi north on OR-31. Worth it if you want the drive, or catch it tomorrow on the way out
  • Keep it light. A long soak and an afternoon nap is the right call before an all-nighter
Carry a paper map. Cell coverage across Lake County is unreliable to nonexistent. Download offline maps too, and tell someone your plan.

Route

Marymere Falls (1.8 mi round trip) from Lake Crescent first thing, then east to Port Angeles and 17 miles up Hurricane Ridge Road to 5,242 ft — the Bailey Range and Mount Olympus in front of you, the Strait of Juan de Fuca and Vancouver Island behind.
